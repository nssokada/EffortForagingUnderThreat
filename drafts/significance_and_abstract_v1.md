## Significance Statement (~175 words)

> Animals foraging in the wild — and humans navigating modern threats — must integrate the energetic cost of action against the probability of failure. Decades of research have studied these two costs in parallel: anxiety research has focused on threat sensitivity, while motivation research has focused on effort cost. Yet how the brain combines them into a single embodied decision under risk has remained formally uncharacterized. In two preregistered samples (N = 571), we show that humans integrate threat and effort via a single embodied value computation in which the two cost types produce structurally distinct architectures: capture cost — which couples outcome to motor effort through survival probability — dissociates across decision and motor channels, driving cautious choice but vigorous motor commitment, while effort cost — which is directly produced by motor output — aligns across both, suppressing decision and execution equally. This integration generates a quantitatively predictable population-level coupling between behaviors and reveals three orthogonal embodied dimensions of adaptive defense, providing a computational foundation for understanding how the body translates threat-relevant information into survival-relevant action.

## Abstract (~295 words)

> Every survival decision under risk requires the body to integrate the energetic cost of action against the probability of failure. These two costs — captured separately in the parallel literatures on threat sensitivity (anxiety research) and effort cost (motivation research) — have not been formally integrated into a single embodied computation. Here we provide such an account, validated in two preregistered samples (exploratory N = 290; confirmatory N = 281), using a foraging-under-predation task in which participants choose between effort-cost options and execute calibrated motor commitment to escape attacks. A hierarchical Bayesian fitness function W(u) with two per-subject parameters — capture cost (ω) and effort cost (κ) — jointly fits decision and motor execution under one likelihood, beating simpler alternatives by ΔWAIC ≈ 1,600–4,700 in both samples. The two parameters integrate via structurally distinct architectures: ω is dissociated across channels (β = −0.15 on choice, +0.13 on vigor — avoidance with motor mobilization), while κ is aligned (β = −0.07 on choice, −0.24 on vigor — suppression on both). This sign asymmetry generates a quantitatively predicted population-level coupling between choice and motor vigor — predicted r = +0.143 / +0.052 vs observed +0.150 / +0.077, matching within 0.025 in both samples with no free parameters at the marginal level. Polar decomposition of (ω, κ) reveals three orthogonal embodied dimensions — strategic style of avoidance, intensity of avoidance, and accuracy of affective calibration — each independently predicting adaptive performance (β = −0.32, −0.22, +0.18 on optimality), with distinct metacognitive signatures (style determines subjective confidence; intensity determines accuracy of the affective signal). The framework bridges separately developed anxiety and effort literatures, formalizes survival-relevant decision-making in embodied behavior, and reveals a three-dimensional individual-difference architecture of defensive cognition that single-channel research cannot detect.
