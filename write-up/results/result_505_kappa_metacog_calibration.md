# Result 505 — κ (effort cost) predicts metacognitive calibration accuracy

> **⚠️ Exploratory. Numbers from `instructions/memory/hypotheses.md` § H20 (deprecated k parameter; current κ analog expected to behave similarly).**

## Result (legacy, using deprecated k)

| Test | r | p |
|---|---|---|
| k × anxiety threat calibration | −0.309 | < 0.001 |
| k × confidence threat calibration | −0.210 | < 0.001 |
| Joint model for anxiety calibration ~ z + k + β | R² = 0.121 | — |
| (driven by k, β = −0.342) | | |

## Interpretation

People who discount effort more (high k) show *worse* calibration of their affect ratings to objective threat conditions. The substantive reading: k captures task engagement. Low-k subjects are more engaged across all three channels — they choose carefully, press hard, and report more differentiated affect across conditions. High-k subjects discount effort, press less, and also report less differentiated (less calibrated) affect.

This is a transdiagnostic-relevant finding: the *same parameter* (κ in current framework) that governs motor execution governs the quality of subjective monitoring. It suggests that "apathy"-like phenotypes (low engagement) manifest simultaneously across decision, execution, and metacognition.

The result is from the deprecated framework but the substantive claim is expected to survive in the M4 framework with κ replacing k.
