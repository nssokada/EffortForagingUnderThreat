# Result 401 — Marginal r(choice, vigor) is quantitatively predicted by cancellation of ω-dissociated and κ-aligned channel pathways

> **Supersedes the legacy H29 framing.** An earlier version of result_401 reported `r(p_heavy, mean_pre_encounter_vigor) = −0.018` from the legacy internal hypothesis tree, with a vigor metric that was *capacity-normalised and choice-ratio-adjusted on the pre-encounter window only*. This rewrite uses the M4-consistent vigor metric (cell-mean aggregate, the same quantity decomposed in [[result_208]]) so that 401's marginal correlation lives on the same scale as the partial slopes that predict it. Under this operationalisation the marginal r is **small positive (+0.150 / +0.077)**, not null, and the embodied W(u) framework predicts it quantitatively.

## Overview

The marginal correlation between subject-mean P(heavy) and subject-mean pressing rate across subjects is small and positive: r = +0.150 (p = 0.011) in the exploratory sample and r = +0.077 (p = 0.201) in the confirmatory sample. Under the embodied W(u) framework, this marginal correlation is not a free statistical fact — it is *generated* by the four partial slopes that map (ω, κ) onto each behavioural channel (reported in [[result_208]]) plus the population correlation between ω and κ. The ω-pathway pulls r negative (β_ωc · β_ωv < 0, because ω has *dissociated* effects: avoidance on choice but mobilised execution on vigor); the κ-pathway pulls r positive (β_κc · β_κv > 0, because κ has *aligned* effects: effort cost suppresses both); these nearly cancel, and the residual is filled in by a cross term driven by the moderate positive correlation between ω and κ. The resulting predicted marginal is +0.143 (exploratory) and +0.052 (confirmatory). **Both predictions match the observed sample correlations to within 0.025**, in both samples, with no free parameters at the marginal level. The marginal coupling is therefore a derived consequence of two parameters acting via four signed pathways, not an independent statistical fact about the dataset.

## Hypothesis

**Statement.** If choice and vigor are both generated by the embodied W(u) framework using the same per-subject (ω, κ) — with ω weighting the survival-dependent term that couples motor output to capture probability, and κ weighting the energetic cost of effort — then the marginal correlation between subject-mean P(heavy) and subject-mean pressing rate across subjects is *not* a free quantity. It is determined by the four channel-specific partial slopes and the population correlation between ω and κ, and can be computed without ever looking at the marginal.

**Predicted direction.** The ω-pathway contribution `β_ωc · β_ωv` is negative because β_ωc < 0 (capture-averse subjects avoid heavy) but β_ωv > 0 (capture-averse subjects press harder when committed — the embodied dissociated signature). The κ-pathway contribution `β_κc · β_κv` is positive because β_κc < 0 and β_κv < 0 (effort-averse subjects avoid heavy *and* press less — the aligned signature). The two pathways cancel approximately, leaving a small residual whose sign and magnitude depend on the cross term `r(ω, κ) · (β_ωc · β_κv + β_κc · β_ωv)`. Predicted marginal r is small in magnitude with sign determined by the residual + cross term.

**Preregistered criterion.** Not in the current preregistration's formal H# list. A stale reference to "H1d choice-vigor shift independence (a null test)" remains in the sample-size rationale ([preregistration.md:141](../preregistration.md#L141)) — leftover from an earlier draft where H1d existed as a formal hypothesis. Internal-history equivalent is H29 (`instructions/memory/hypotheses.md` § H29), the parent claim of the 400-block dissociation framework. The qualitative criterion for the rewrite is that (a) the parameter-mediated prediction matches the observed marginal r in *both* samples within reasonable tolerance, and (b) the sign of each pathway contribution replicates across samples.

**Source of the hypothesis.** Under embodied W(u), choice and vigor share their per-subject parameterisation. The marginal correlation between the two behaviours is therefore not free — it is a derived population-level quantity. This is a strong test of the embodiment claim: any account in which choice and vigor are driven by different traits (channel-independent), or by a single common drive (single-drive activation), produces a different marginal-r prediction. The current result tests whether the specific embodied structure of W(u) (one parameter operating on the outcome-coupled survival term, the other on the output-cost effort term) generates the marginal r the data exhibit.

## Method

The result combines two computations:

**(1) Observed marginal r.** For each sample, compute the across-subjects Pearson correlation between `p_heavy` (mean heavy-choice indicator, 0–1) and `mean_vigor` (mean pressing rate across condition-cell means). Report r and the two-sided p-value.

**(2) Predicted marginal r from partial slopes.** Under z-scored predictors `ω_z` and `κ_z` with `Var(ω_z) = Var(κ_z) = 1` and `Cov(ω_z, κ_z) = r(ω, κ)`, the population covariance between choice and vigor decomposes as:

`Cov_param(choice, vigor) = β_ωc · β_ωv + β_κc · β_κv + r(ω, κ) · (β_ωc · β_κv + β_κc · β_ωv)`

The three terms correspond to the ω-pathway, the κ-pathway, and the cross term respectively. The partial slopes are extracted from main-effects-only Cartesian regressions on each channel — `p_heavy ~ ω_z + κ_z` and `mean_vigor ~ ω_z + κ_z` — fit by `bambi` with `4 chains × 2,000 draws + 1,000 tuning`, weakly informative default priors (matches the 208 decomposition configuration exactly, cached in `config.BKW`). Dividing `Cov_param` by `SD(choice) · SD(vigor)` (both measured directly on the marginal sample) gives the predicted marginal r.

**Crucially, the marginal correlation is never seen during fitting.** The partial slopes are fit per-channel; the marginal r is derived. If the embodied framework were wrong — for example, if there were an unmeasured third parameter ξ driving both channels, or if choice and vigor were not actually generated by a common (ω, κ) — the predicted marginal would diverge from the observed marginal.

**Script:** `scripts/analysis/h4_choice_decomp.py`. Mirrors the post-hoc decomposition methodology of [`notebooks/analysis/H4_profiles_optimality.ipynb`](../../notebooks/analysis/H4_profiles_optimality.ipynb) (cell at ~lines 230–275), extended to include `p_heavy` as a fourth outcome and to compute the predicted-vs-observed r comparison. Convergence diagnostics across all 12 fits: R̂ = 1.000, ESS_bulk ≥ 7,001. Each fit ≈ 1 s wall time under NumPyro on CPU.

## Result

**Observed marginal r in both samples:**

| Sample | N | r(P(heavy), mean_vigor) | p (two-sided) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exploratory | 290 | **+0.150** | 0.011 |
| Confirmatory | 281 | **+0.077** | 0.201 |

The exploratory sample shows a small but statistically reliable positive coupling; the confirmatory sample shows a directionally consistent positive coupling that does not reach the conventional threshold (p > 0.05). Both samples are well within the small-positive regime — neither is null in direction, and neither exhibits the moderate-to-strong positive correlation a single-drive activation account would predict.

**Pathway decomposition under the embodied framework:**

| Quantity | Exploratory (N=290) | Confirmatory (N=281) |
|---|---|---|
| β_ωc (ω → P(heavy)) | −0.154 | −0.167 |
| β_κc (κ → P(heavy)) | −0.075 | −0.058 |
| β_ωv (ω → mean_vigor) | +0.134 | +0.122 |
| β_κv (κ → mean_vigor) | −0.245 | −0.233 |
| r(ω_z, κ_z) | +0.369 | +0.302 |
| SD(P(heavy)) | 0.2024 | 0.2049 |
| SD(mean_vigor) | 0.2741 | 0.2612 |
| **ω-pathway Cov** = β_ωc · β_ωv | **−0.0206** | **−0.0204** |
| **κ-pathway Cov** = β_κc · β_κv | **+0.0184** | **+0.0135** |
| Cross-term Cov = r(ω,κ) · (β_ωc · β_κv + β_κc · β_ωv) | +0.0102 | +0.0096 |
| **Total Cov_param** | **+0.0079** | **+0.0028** |

The ω-pathway is consistently ≈ −0.020 in both samples (negative because ω has dissociated effects across channels: β_ωc < 0, β_ωv > 0). The κ-pathway is +0.013 to +0.018 (positive because κ has aligned effects: β_κc < 0 and β_κv < 0). The two pathways nearly cancel — their sum is approximately −0.002 in exploratory and −0.007 in confirmatory — leaving a small residual that the cross term (positive because ω and κ are themselves positively correlated in the population) tips into the small-positive regime.

**Predicted vs observed marginal r:**

| Sample | r_predicted | r_observed | |r_pred − r_obs| |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exploratory | **+0.143** | +0.150 | 0.007 |
| Confirmatory | **+0.052** | +0.077 | 0.025 |

The embodied W(u) framework predicts the observed marginal correlation to within 0.007 in the exploratory sample and 0.025 in the confirmatory sample. The prediction was derived from inputs (partial slopes, parameter correlation, marginal SDs) measured *without* looking at the marginal correlation. **Verdict:** the parameter-mediated prediction matches the data in both samples.

## Interpretation

**The marginal coupling is generated, not free.** The 401 result is not "choice and vigor happen to be uncorrelated" or "choice and vigor happen to be weakly correlated" — it is "the marginal correlation is the specific small value the embodied framework requires given the partial slopes and the parameter correlation." If the marginal were +0.40, the framework would be falsified. If the marginal were −0.20, the framework would be falsified. If the predicted and observed values were close in one sample but not the other, the framework would be on shaky ground. Instead, the prediction matches within 0.025 in both samples. This is the kind of constraint satisfaction that only obtains when the two channels really do share their parameterisation through a structured value function.

**The mechanism is asymmetric: ω is dissociated across channels, κ is aligned across channels.** Both parameters operate on both behaviours, but they operate *differently*:

- **ω = avoidance / capture cost.** β_ωc < 0 — capture-averse subjects choose heavy less often. β_ωv > 0 — capture-averse subjects press *harder* during execution. The same single parameter produces opposite-signed expressions on the two channels. This is not a contradiction — it is what W(u) requires. Higher ω makes every unit of survival probability more valuable, and pressing harder raises survival probability via the speed function in S(u). So the same disposition that drives strategic avoidance also drives motor mobilisation when execution is in progress. ω is *avoidance* throughout; its motor expression looks like fighting harder because the body is trying to escape the high-stakes capture penalty.

- **κ = activation / effort cost.** β_κc < 0 and β_κv < 0 — effort-averse subjects choose heavy less often *and* press less. Same parameter, same-signed expression on both channels. κ enters as a unidirectional suppression because the energetic cost term `κ · (u − req)² · D` penalises high pressing rates and the demand cost `κ · req · D` penalises heavy options. There is no outcome-coupling that would invert κ's sign on either channel.

The cancellation in `r(choice, vigor)` is therefore a *structural* consequence of one parameter (ω) operating on the outcome-coupled survival term while the other (κ) operates on the output-cost effort term. A model in which both parameters operated the same way on both channels — for example, two effort-cost parameters — could not produce this pattern. A model with a single parameter could not produce four signed pathways at all.

**The prediction discriminates between three classes of theories.** Three natural alternatives to the embodied W(u) framework make different predictions for marginal r(choice, vigor):

1. **Single-drive "general activation"** accounts (one latent activation factor driving both behaviours, à la BIS/BAS or undifferentiated arousal) predict `r > 0` of substantial magnitude. The observed +0.15 / +0.08 is too small for this class of theory, and the cancellation mechanism is structurally incompatible: a single drive cannot produce opposite-signed effects on the motor channel from ω versus κ.
2. **Channel-independent** accounts (choice and vigor are governed by separate, unrelated systems) predict `r ≈ 0` *with no specific mechanism* and no way to generate the specific cross-term residual the data show. The framework would be consistent with the small marginal r but would be silent about why it is that specific value.
3. **Embodied W(u)** (two parameters operating via four signed partial pathways, with ω dissociated across channels and κ aligned) predicts both the near-cancellation *and* the specific small positive residual driven by the cross term. The quantitative match in both samples is what selects this account from the three.

**The four downstream interpretive uses** the 401 result anchors:

1. **Cross-channel parameter coherence (anchors the embodiment argument in [[result_207]]).** 207 argues that the joint likelihood is necessary because (ω, κ) need to be identified jointly across both channels. 401 supplies the population-level empirical evidence that the joint identification produces meaningful cross-channel structure: the marginal r between behaviours generated by (ω, κ) matches the marginal r the data exhibit. Without 401, the joint-likelihood argument is methodological — with 401 it has a quantitative population-level anchor.

2. **The threat-resolved sign reversal in [[result_404]] becomes interpretable as shifting pathway weights.** 404 reports `r(P(heavy), vigor)` flipping from +0.196 at T = 0.1 to −0.219 at T = 0.9. Under the 401 cancellation framework, this is the ω-pathway becoming progressively more dominant as threat rises: higher T raises the marginal value of pressing harder via the survival term, increasing β_ωv (the dissociated motor expression of ω), which tips the cancellation toward the ω-side and flips the marginal toward negative. 401 supplies the generative interpretation that 404's threat-resolved pattern requires.

3. **Subject-level deviation analyses ([[result_405]], [[result_506]]) become dimensional rather than categorical.** Rather than median-splitting into HL / LH / HH / LL quadrants, the embodied frame supports a continuous reading: subjects whose (P(heavy), mean_vigor) joint position deviates strongly from the parameter-predicted joint distribution have specific metacognitive signatures (overconfidence, miscalibration). The deviation has a model-derived expected value; subjects far from that value are interpretable as having either large residuals (noisy execution or context-dependent decision/execution dissociation) or parameter values that the M4 fit did not capture cleanly. The dimensional framing replaces the four-phenotype categorical framing.

## Caveats & Limitations

- **The confirmatory observed r is not statistically reliable** (r = +0.077, p = 0.201). The prediction `r_predicted = +0.052` is also small, so the match is "near-zero matches near-zero" rather than a sharp distributional test. The exploratory match (predicted +0.143 vs observed +0.150, within 0.007) is the tighter constraint. Both samples are consistent with the embodied prediction, but the test is most informative in the exploratory.

- **Main-effects-only partial slopes used in the predicted-r calculation.** The Cartesian regressions with the ω × κ interaction term give slightly different β values (e.g., β_ωv = +0.137 with interaction vs +0.134 main-effects-only in exploratory). The difference is within rounding for the qualitative conclusion but matters for the precise predicted-r value. The full interaction model would also require terms in the covariance formula that are negligible under approximately joint-normal (ω, κ).

- **The framework's prediction is well-defined only for the operationalisation it inherits its partial slopes from.** Specifically: the prediction holds when `mean_vigor` is the M4 cell-mean aggregate that 208 decomposes. Under the legacy operationalisation (pre-encounter capacity-normalised, choice-ratio-adjusted), the marginal r is different (r ≈ −0.018 from H29) because the metric has been residualised against choice composition. A separate derivation would be needed to predict marginal r under the legacy metric. This is not a contradiction — it is a feature of the operationalisation choice.

- **Residual cross-channel covariance is not formally modeled.** The formula `Cov_param = β_ωc·β_ωv + ...` captures the parameter-mediated component of `Cov(choice, vigor)` but not any residual covariance `Cov(ε_c, ε_v)` between the two regression residuals. If there were substantial residual covariance — for example, from trial-level dependencies that the subject-aggregated regressions miss — the predicted r and observed r could differ by exactly that residual. The match within 0.025 in both samples bounds the residual cross-channel covariance to be small at the population level.

- **Causal interpretation is constrained.** The 401 result establishes that the marginal correlation is parameter-mediated under the embodied framework, but does not establish that the parameters are *causal* drivers of the behaviours in any stronger sense than the regression structure already implies. Causal claims about ω-vs-κ contributions to specific subject behaviours require the trial-level analyses in [[result_404]] (threat-resolved coupling) and [[result_502]] (anxiety calibration).

- **Confirmatory replication of result_405 and result_506 under the dimensional framing is deferred.** Those results currently use four-quadrant median splits; reframing them dimensionally (subjects' deviation from the parameter-predicted (choice, vigor) joint expectation as a continuous predictor of metacognitive outcomes) is a separate analytical task. Their substantive findings (HL overconfidence, LH calibration; miscalibration tracks dissociation) likely survive the reframe but the dimensional analysis has not yet been run.
